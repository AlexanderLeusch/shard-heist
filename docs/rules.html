Game rules

These documents describe what the game does, as implemented in the code. When a rule changes, update the document, the code and the tests together, then run npm run docs to rebuild the printable version.

The game has two rooms that are played one after the other. Until the group connects them, each room is its own page.

The whole game in one paragraph

In April 2012 the Starfall necklace, lent by the Ashford family to Lisbon's naval museum for the centenary of the Titanic, vanished from its case. You were the detective on the case, until the trail ended at your own door. Now you are inmate 4471, convicted of stealing it. Room 1: you have ten minutes in a prison washroom to find the code for a hatch in the floor. If you get out (Ending A), a note shows that the guard wanted you out, and a car is waiting at the end of the tunnel. Room 2: the police chase begins. You drive a getaway vehicle through Lisbon's Baixa, on the highway and across the countryside, collecting star shards while up to four police cars and a helicopter hunt you.

One night, one look

  • Time: both rooms are set on the same night: room 1 around 02:15, room 2 from about 02:30.
  • Design: both rooms share one design system: the same background, glass panels, fonts, buttons and cards. Each room keeps its own accent colour: orange in the prison, cyan on the run. Hale's radio messages in room 2 are orange because he comes from room 1.
  • A test (tests/page.test.js) checks that the shared design tokens and the story links between the rooms stay in place.

How the two rooms will connect (planned, not built)

  • Ending A of room 1 shows a button that opens room 2.
  • Ending B of room 1 stays in room 1 (try again).
  • Open question for the group: does room 2 then start in free ride, or straight in the story mode The Necklace Job?

Room 1 · Plot Twist: Cell Block D

This document describes the rules in escape-room/src/rules.js. Values marked (open) are not decided yet.

Goal

Open the hatch in the floor of the toilet stall and climb out before the clock reaches zero.

Story

The briefing is a short series of cards; Next and Back move between them.

  1. The front page. The Lisbon Times of Monday 16 April 2012: the Starfall necklace, lent by the Ashford family to the Museu de Marinha for an exhibition marking 100 years since the Titanic sank, vanished from its glass case on the night of the centenary. No alarm sounded, and a detective was put on the case.
  2. Who you are. Three weeks ago you were that detective. The trail ended at your own door: the necklace's velvet box was hidden in your car. Tonight you are inmate 4471, convicted of the theft: someone framed you. Your oldest friend on the force kept digging and found the proof that clears your name, but the people who framed you came after him. Before he disappeared from Lisbon, he hid the evidence across the city, in places only you would know to look.
  3. The door. Officer Hale walks you to washroom 2. In a short scene in the room you turn round to the open door, see him in the corridor light, and watch him swing the door shut and lock it. Through the door he says: "Your ten minutes have begun. Not a second more." He waits outside.
  4. The rumour. Inmates whisper about a hatch in the floor of this washroom, locked with a four-digit code that is hidden somewhere in the room.
  5. How to play: the controls.

The clock only starts when the player presses Start the clock after the briefing.

Clues and the code

  • The code is 1912, the year the Titanic sank. It is the same in every game (open: should it change between games?).
  • Players who do not know the history can still solve it: "sailed only once" gives the missing digit 1.
  • Every clue you read is kept in your clue notes (3 at most). Reading a clue again does not count twice.
  • The crooked tile comes off by hand and hides the car key, not a clue.

The lock

  • Four wheels, each showing 0–9. Up after 9 is 0, down after 0 is 9.
  • You can only turn the wheels and try the code while kneeling at the hatch.
  • Try the code:

- Right code: the hatch opens, Ending A. - Wrong code: "Clunk. The lock holds." Hale hears the noise, and you lose 30 seconds. The number of wrong codes is counted.

Endings

A · Out belowThe right code before time runs outYou climb down the ladder. A note is taped to it: "Took you long enough. A car is waiting at the end of the tunnel. — H." The guard wanted you out. That is the plot twist that leads to room 2.
B · Back to the cellThe clock reaches 0:00 (after the extra minute, if the door was jammed)The door bangs open and Hale and two officers take you back to cell 4471.

The earlier decision: jamming the door early gives one more minute at the very end. That can turn Ending B into Ending A.

After an ending, every action is refused except Play again / Try again, which starts a new game from the briefing.

Saving

  • The game is saved in the browser after every accepted action and every few seconds while the clock runs.
  • After a reload, the player can Continue (with the time that was left) or start a New game, which asks for confirmation first.
  • A saved game is checked field by field when loaded. Anything that is not a real save is ignored, and a new game starts.

Room 2 · Shard Heist

This document describes the rules in src/core.js, and the pickups and police in src/game.js.

Where room 2 starts

Room 2 begins where room 1 ends. You (inmate 4471, the framed detective) have climbed out of the tunnel under Cell Block D. It is about 02:30 at night. In a lock-up garage, Officer Hale's getaway vehicle is waiting. Every police car in town is now hunting the escaped inmate.

Officer Hale is a secret ally. He believes you were framed, but he cannot prove it. In the story mode he helps over the radio; his messages are shown in orange, the colour of room 1. Police dispatch messages are red.

Two ways to play

  • Free ride: pick a map and a getaway vehicle, then escape.
  • *Story mode, The Necklace Job:* three acts on the three maps, in order, with a story and three endings.

  • A shard or a piece of evidence is picked up within 3.8 m; a repair kit within 3.6 m, and only when you have damage.
  • Lisbon has traffic lights, 26 other cars, two white city buses, four motorcycles and three yellow trams. The trams only run on their tracks, a stone track bed with two rails set into the road: a clockwise loop through Rua da Conceição, Rua da Prata, the north side of the Praça do Comércio and Rua Áurea. As on real Lisbon streets, cars and the player can drive over the tracks. The buses are 12 m long, so at a crossing they go straight on or turn left in the wide arc, never right into the tight corner (unless that is the only way left at the edge of the map). Everything stops at red lights and keeps its distance to the back of the vehicle ahead, so nobody runs into the long tail of a bus or a tram. On the highway traffic drives both ways: cars, SUVs, lorries, three coaches and six motorcycles; lorries and coaches keep to the middle and slow lanes. On country roads there is oncoming traffic, with one coach and three motorcycles among it, and driving off the road cuts your top speed to 55 %.
  • Other vehicles at night: every vehicle throws its headlights on the road ahead, so a car waiting at a dark crossing can be seen from far away. Its bright brake lights are on while it slows down or stands. You can crash into buses and motorcycles like into any other vehicle.
  • Each map has an edge you cannot cross. When you near it the screen turns red and a "turn back" arrow appears. In Lisbon the outer streets are part of the map, so there this only happens when you head for the edge.

The evidence in Lisbon

In Lisbon you do not collect star shards. Your oldest friend on the force found out who really broke into the naval museum and hid the proof across the Baixa (room 1 tells this in its briefing). There are six pieces, one of each, in random places every run. Each floats above the street with a glow, and the panel at the top left shows one icon per piece that lights up when it is in the car.

EvidenceWhat it proves
PhotosA figure at the museum window at 01:40 on the night of the theft, while you were at home
FingerprintsFingerprints and DNA traces from the display case, and they are not yours
TorchThe thief's torch, dropped in the museum garden, with paint from the window frame on it
LockpickThe museum's lock was opened with these picks, not forced
ClothesThe dark clothes from the photos, with glass splinters from the display case in the seams
BalaclavaThe balaclava from the photos, with hairs inside that are not yours

Before you drive a map of the Baixa shows where the six pieces lie, with their names, the repair kits, your start and the gate. The clock waits until you press Start driving. While you drive an arrow on the screen (at the bottom in the middle; on an upright phone under the pursuit panel) points to the nearest piece still out there, with its name and distance; once all six are found it turns gold and points to the witness, and once she is in the car, to the gate. (The story's sealed letter is on neither.)

The witness. With all six pieces in the car, one thing is still missing: a person. Marta Silva, a night-shift nurse, saw the real thief climb out of the museum window, and she will say so in court. She waits on a pavement somewhere in the Baixa, 70 to 200 m from where you are, waving, with a golden light over her head. The radar shows her in gold (an arrow at the rim while she is out of range), the signal bars point to her, and a seventh icon appears in the panel. Stop next to her, closer than 7 m and slower than about 11 km/h, and she walks to the passenger door and gets in. Drive off before she reaches the car (more than 12 m away) and she goes back to wait. With her on board, the gate under the Arco da Rua Augusta opens. The police keep chasing you while you wait for her.

The finale. Drive through the gate and the picture fades: you are on the 25 de Abril bridge with Marta beside you, driving south over the Tejo at about 86 km/h while the first light of the morning comes up in the east. There is nothing more to do: no police, no helicopter, heroic music (brass, strings and timpani, about 14 seconds, on the music volume). Then the result card. The drive over the bridge does not count towards your time. In the story, it ends act 1.

The highway and the countryside keep their bonus shards (+4 s each).

The city map: Lisbon's Baixa

The Baixa is the centre of Lisbon, rebuilt as a street grid after the 1755 earthquake. The map is a simplified version of it: the blocks are square, and the grid has 7 streets each way. The real Baixa has a few more, so some are left out. The real Baixa is also flat; the hills on this map are a game decision.

DirectionStreets, in their real order
West to eastRua do Crucifixo, Rua Áurea, Rua dos Sapateiros, Rua Augusta, Rua dos Correeiros, Rua da Prata, Rua dos Fanqueiros
North to southRua de Santa Justa, Rua da Vitória, Rua de São Nicolau, Rua da Conceição, Rua do Comércio, Praça do Comércio (north side), Avenida Infante Dom Henrique
  • In free ride, the panel at the top left shows the street or square you are on. In story mode it shows the act instead.
  • You start on Rua Augusta, facing south towards the Arco da Rua Augusta.
  • Squares: the Rossio and the Praça da Figueira in the north, the Praça do Comércio by the river in the south. The statue on the Praça do Comércio stands in the middle of the road, so traffic passes on both sides.
  • Landmarks you can see but not reach: the river Tejo, the 25 de Abril bridge (you only cross it in the finale), Cristo Rei and the castle hill. The river moves: long swells and fine ripples, it mirrors the sky towards the horizon, and the lights of the far bank tremble on it as long streaks. The Santa Justa lift stands on the map, and you can crash into it.
  • Parks: there are three, which the real Baixa does not have. The story needs them for the sealed letter.
  • Houses: closed blocks of Pombaline houses, wall to wall, as the Baixa was rebuilt after 1755: a ground floor plus 3 to 5 floors (OpenStreetMap gives most buildings 5 levels), arched stone ground floors with old shop fronts, the tallest windows and the balconies on the first floor, limewash colours, and tile roofs with dormers. Tiled fronts are the exception, as they are in the real Baixa.
  • Real buildings in their place: the National Theatre and Rossio station north of the Rossio, the yellow arcades and corner towers of the Praça do Comércio, and the Sé, seen at the end of Rua da Conceição. Their position is simplified, and they are modelled freely, not measured.
  • It is always about 02:30 at night. The clock on the arch shows it.

Hills. All three maps have hills, up to about 20 % steep. The Arco, the river front and the two ends of the highway are level. Uphill your top speed falls by up to 16 % and gravity pulls you back, half as strongly as it pushes you downhill. Downhill your top speed rises by up to 22 % and you coast on. The truck feels slopes most, the motorcycle least. Brakes and rolling friction hold a stopped vehicle in place.

Smooth picture. If the game runs below 48 frames per second for 2 seconds, it lowers the resolution one step (85, 70, then 60 %). If that does not help, the step is taken back. After 20 seconds at 57 or more it goes back up, at most twice. The choice of Ultra or Balanced stays yours.

Helicopter. After 60 seconds on the run (or sooner with the fifth star), a police helicopter joins the chase for the rest of the run and hunts you with its searchlight. Its searchlight follows you at 94 % of the police speed. While you are in the light (within 9 m of its centre), the police drive 6 % faster, and in the city they see you from 170 m instead of 110 m. It cannot bust you or damage you. An undamaged car or motorcycle at full speed on a clear, level road stays ahead of the light; the truck, and a badly damaged car, cannot.

Radio

Seven stations, or off: Reggae, Techno, Hardstyle, Schlager, Sing-Along Pop, Classical and Heavy Metal. Choose in the menu, or at any time with R or the radio button. The music plays while you drive, and is silent in the menu and in pause.

All of it is composed and synthesised by the game, with no music files. The tunes are generated by the game. The classical station plays Bach and Beethoven, whose works are in the public domain.

What is remembered, and what happens to bad values

Every stored value comes from outside the program, so each one is checked when it is loaded. A value that is missing or wrong is ignored and the default is used; the game never breaks because of it.
