The whole game in one paragraph

In April 2012 the Starfall necklace, lent by the Ashford family to Lisbon's naval museum for the centenary of the Titanic, vanished from its case. You were the detective on the case, until the trail ended at your own door. Now you are inmate 4471, convicted of stealing it. Room 1: you have ten minutes in a prison washroom to find the code for a hatch in the floor. If you get out (Ending A), a note shows that the guard wanted you out, and a car is waiting at the end of the tunnel. Room 2: the police chase begins. You drive a getaway vehicle through Lisbon's Baixa, on the highway and across the countryside, collecting star shards while up to four police cars and a helicopter hunt you.

One night, one look

  • Time: both rooms are set on the same night: room 1 around 02:15, room 2 from about 02:30.
  • Design: both rooms share one design system: the same background, glass panels, fonts, buttons and cards. Each room keeps its own accent colour: orange in the prison, cyan on the run. Hale's radio messages in room 2 are orange because he comes from room 1.
  • A test (tests/project/page.test.js) checks that the shared design tokens and the story links between the rooms stay in place.

How the two rooms will connect (planned, not built)

  • Ending A of room 1 shows a button that opens room 2.
  • Ending B of room 1 stays in room 1 (try again).
  • Open question for the group: does room 2 then start in free ride, or straight in the story mode The Necklace Job?

Room 1 · Plot Twist: Cell Block D

This document describes the rules in room-1-escape/js/rules.js. Values marked (open) are not decided yet.

Goal

Open the hatch in the floor of the toilet stall and climb out before the clock reaches zero.

Story

The briefing is a short series of cards; Next and Back move between them.

  1. The front page. The Lisbon Times of Monday 16 April 2012: the Starfall necklace, lent by the Ashford family to the Museu de Marinha for an exhibition marking 100 years since the Titanic sank, vanished from its glass case on the night of the centenary. No alarm sounded, and a detective was put on the case.
  2. Who you are. Three weeks ago you were that detective. The trail ended at your own door: the necklace's velvet box was hidden in your car. Tonight you are inmate 4471, convicted of the theft: someone framed you. Your oldest friend on the force kept digging and found the proof that clears your name, but the people who framed you came after him. Before he disappeared from Lisbon, he hid the evidence across the city, in places only you would know to look.
  3. The door. Officer Hale walks you to washroom 2. In a short scene in the room you turn round to the open door, see him in the corridor light, and watch him swing the door shut and lock it. Through the door he says: "Your ten minutes have begun. Not a second more." He waits outside.
  4. The rumour. Inmates whisper about a hatch in the floor of this washroom, locked with a four-digit code. Three spots in the room hide a car key, the first two digits and the last two digits of the code, each behind a short Titanic mini game.
  5. How to play: the controls.

The clock only starts when the player presses Start the clock after the briefing.

You walk freely through the washroom in the first person. Where you stand decides your scene: the stall (inside it or right in front of its doorway), the sinks (the west end of the room) or the door (everywhere else). You have to be well inside a new part of the room before it counts, so walking along a border never switches back and forth. The description of a scene is shown the first time you walk in. The Hatch is the only scene you do not walk into: in the stall you click the hatch (or its marker) to kneel down at the lock, and any step stands you up again. Walls, the sink counter, the bin, the bucket, the stall partitions, its open door and the toilet block your way.

You see your own hands at the bottom of the picture: real hand models with a joint in every finger, in the black and sand stripes of the prison uniform. At rest they hang relaxed and never quite still: they breathe, drift a little, and now and then make a small gesture of their own (a loose fist, a tap of the index finger, a stretch of the fingers, the thumb rubbing the fingertips). As you walk, the arms swing like in a real walk, opposite to each other, every swing a little different; the wrists trail behind the arms and the fingers close a little on the back swing and open on the forward swing. Your head dips at every footfall, a little more or less with each step, and sways gently from one foot to the other. You hear every footfall: real recordings of shoes on the hard floor, a splash when you step into a puddle and boots on the wooden rungs of the ladder, each step a little different, with the echo of the tiled room. The arms trail behind when you turn your head, and the right hand opens and reaches out whenever you use something. If the hand models cannot load (for example when the page is opened straight from the folder), simpler drawn hands stand in. They never disappear into a wall, and they are hidden during the endings. They change nothing in the rules.

On a laptop or desktop you only walk with the arrow keys; there are no buttons for it. A touch screen has no arrow keys, so there (and only there) "Walk to" buttons glide you to a fixed spot in another scene. Walking anywhere else is refused: "You cannot get there from here."

Clues and the code

There are exactly three clues, one behind each of three spots. Clicking a spot opens a mini game of at most 30 seconds. Winning it gives the clue; losing it (or running out of the 30 seconds) gives nothing, and the same game can be tried again at once. Giving up (Esc or Give up), pausing or an ending stops the game without a result.

SpotWhereConditionClue when won
The crooked tileThe Stall–The tile comes off and the car key lies behind it (take it with "Car key behind the tile")
Writing on the mirrorThe SinksOnly while the hot tap runs and steams the mirror up. Without steam you only see your face, and no game opens.The first two digits: "UNSINKABLE? 19 _ _" appears in the steam
Scratches by the doorThe Door–The last two digits: "_ _ 1 2" is gouged into the wall
  • The code is 1912, the year the Titanic sank. It is the same in every game.
  • A won clue is kept in your clue notes and can be read again at its spot; its mini game cannot be played again.
  • The graffiti on the stall wall ("She was unsinkable. She sailed only once. The year opens the floor.") is a hint, not one of the three clues.

The seven mini games

At the start of every game, three different games out of seven are dealt at random to the three spots (tile, mirror, scratches), so every round plays differently. The rules check that the three are different; the room deals them (MiniGames.pickGames in room-1-escape/js/minigames.js).

GameHow to win in 30 seconds
BattleshipTwo 6 × 6 grids, three ships each (3, 2 and 2 cells). As in the real game, ships never touch, not even at a corner; once a ship is sunk, the water around it is marked and cannot be fired at. Fire at the enemy waters; a hit lets you fire again. Sink all enemy ships before the bot sinks yours. The bot fires at random or, half the time, next to one of its hits.
Spot the differenceTwo pictures of the Titanic at night; the right one has 5 changes, chosen from 9. Click all five, in either picture. A wrong click costs 2 seconds.
Tic-tac-toeYour anchors against the bot's icebergs, you start. Three in a row wins, a draw starts a new round. The bot always takes a winning cell, blocks three times out of four, likes the centre, otherwise plays at random.
Deck runAs a sailor of the Titanic, run to the bow on the icy night of 14 April 1912: first along the side of the ship past its round portholes, then out on the open deck past the railing with its life rings, the lifeboats, a funnel and the foremast. Jump (Space, ↑ or a click) over crates, steamer trunks, barrels and blocks of ice. Each obstacle knocks you down exactly where it is drawn. Three knocks and you are out.
Deck climbAs the same sailor, bounce up through the Titanic: the engine room (boilers, furnaces, pipes), the crew quarters (bunks and lockers), second class (oak-panelled corridors and the library) and first class, the longest level, twice as tall as the others (the Grand Staircase with its glass dome, chandeliers, the clock "Honour and Glory" and the bronze cherub). The game is won the moment you land on the boat deck with its funnels and lifeboats above first class; there is nothing to climb above it. Steer with ← → or the mouse. Lifebuoys bounce you higher. Falling back down loses.
SeagullFly the seagull over the black, icy North Atlantic at night, the lit Titanic in the background; flap with Space, ↑ or a click through ten gaps between stacks of overlapping clouds and icebergs. Only the drawn outline of a cloud or an iceberg counts as a crash.
PinballA vintage White Star Line table in walnut, cream, brass and burgundy. Score 1,500 points with three balls. Flippers: ← and → (A and D), Space for both, or press the left or right half of the table; the ball shoots by itself (or with Space). Points: pop bumper 150, lifebuoy target 250, slingshot 10, each R-M-S lane 100 and all three lanes 1,000 more, and 250 for every flipper shot that reaches the crow's nest bell at the top. A fair player gets there in about 15 seconds; losing all three balls loses.

The lock

  • Four wheels, each showing 0–9. Up after 9 is 0, down after 0 is 9.
  • You can only turn the wheels and try the code while kneeling at the hatch.
  • Try the code:

- Right code: the hatch opens, Ending A. - Wrong code: "Clunk. The lock holds." Hale hears the noise, and you lose 30 seconds. The number of wrong codes is counted.

Saving

  • The game is saved in the browser after every accepted action and every few seconds while the clock runs.
  • After a reload, the player can Continue (with the time that was left) or start a New game, which asks for confirmation first.
  • A saved game is checked field by field when loaded. Anything that is not a real save is ignored, and a new game starts.

Room 2 · Shard Heist

This document describes the rules in room-2-chase/js/core.js, and the pickups and police in room-2-chase/js/game.js.

Where room 2 starts

Room 2 begins where room 1 ends. You (inmate 4471, the framed detective) have climbed out of the tunnel under Cell Block D. It is about 02:30 at night. In a lock-up garage, Officer Hale's getaway vehicle is waiting. Every police car in town is now hunting the escaped inmate.

Officer Hale is a secret ally. He believes you were framed, but he cannot prove it. In the story mode he helps over the radio; his messages are shown in orange, the colour of room 1. Police dispatch messages are red.

Two ways to play

  • Free ride: pick a map and a getaway vehicle, then escape.
  • *Story mode, The Necklace Job:* three acts on the three maps, in order, with a story and three endings.

  • A shard or a piece of evidence is picked up within 3.8 m; a repair kit within 3.6 m, and only when you have damage.
  • Lisbon has traffic lights, 26 other cars, two white city buses, four motorcycles and three yellow trams. The trams only run on their tracks, a stone track bed with two rails set into the road: a clockwise loop through Rua da Conceição, Rua da Prata, the north side of the Praça do Comércio and Rua Áurea. As on real Lisbon streets, cars and the player can drive over the tracks. Each wheel that rolls over a rail gives a jolt: the picture and the vehicle shake, the rail clacks, and phones that can vibrate give a short buzz. The faster you go, the harder the jolt; the front wheels hit first and the rear ones a moment later. Driving along the stone track bed rumbles lightly. The trams themselves run on steel railway wheels with a flange on the inside, on a two-axle truck with springs and brake blocks. The buses are 12 m long, so at a crossing they go straight on or turn left in the wide arc, never right into the tight corner (unless that is the only way left at the edge of the map). Everything stops at red lights and keeps its distance to the back of the vehicle ahead, so nobody runs into the long tail of a bus or a tram. On the highway traffic drives both ways: cars, SUVs, lorries, three coaches and six motorcycles; lorries and coaches keep to the middle and slow lanes. On country roads there is oncoming traffic, with one coach and three motorcycles among it, and driving off the road cuts your top speed to 55 %.
  • Other vehicles at night: every vehicle throws its headlights on the road ahead, so a car waiting at a dark crossing can be seen from far away. Its bright brake lights are on while it slows down or stands. You can crash into buses and motorcycles like into any other vehicle.
  • Each map has an edge you cannot cross. When you near it the screen turns red and a "turn back" arrow appears. In Lisbon the outer streets are part of the map, so there this only happens when you head for the edge.

The evidence in Lisbon

In Lisbon you do not collect star shards. Your oldest friend on the force found out who really broke into the naval museum and hid the proof across the Baixa (room 1 tells this in its briefing). There are six pieces, one of each, in random places every run. Each floats above the street with a glow, and the panel at the top left shows one icon per piece that lights up when it is in the car.

EvidenceWhat it proves

FingerprintsFingerprints and DNA traces from the display case, and they are not yours
TorchThe thief's torch, dropped in the museum garden, with paint from the window frame on it

ClothesThe dark clothes from the photos, with glass splinters from the display case in the seams
BalaclavaThe balaclava from the photos, with hairs inside that are not yours

Before you drive a map of the Baixa shows where the six pieces lie, with their names, the repair kits, your start and the gate. The clock waits until you press Start driving. While you drive an arrow on the screen (at the bottom in the middle; on an upright phone under the pursuit panel) points to the nearest piece still out there, with its name and distance; once all six are found it turns gold and points to the witness, and once she is in the car, to the gate. (The story's sealed letter is on neither.)

The witness. With all six pieces in the car, one thing is still missing: a person. Marta Silva, a night-shift nurse, saw the real thief climb out of the museum window, and she will say so in court. She waits on a pavement somewhere in the Baixa, 70 to 200 m from where you are, with a golden light over her head. She turns to face your car and waves: now and then while you are far away, and without stopping once you are closer than 45 m. The radar shows her in gold (an arrow at the rim while she is out of range), the signal bars point to her, and a seventh icon appears in the panel. Stop next to her, closer than 7 m and slower than about 11 km/h, and she runs to the passenger door on the right. Once the vehicle stands still, she opens the door, steps in (up the steps into the truck's cab), sits down and pulls the door shut; on the motorcycle she climbs onto the pillion seat. Getting in takes about one second, and the vehicle waits for her. From then on she rides along beside you, visible through the window, over the bridge as well. Drive off before she reaches the vehicle (more than 12 m away) and she goes back to wait. With her on board, the golden gate opens at the south-west corner of the Baixa, where Rua do Crucifixo meets Avenida Infante Dom Henrique by the river. It faces the 25 de Abril bridge, and driving through it takes you onto the bridge.

The story card. As soon as Marta has shut the door, the run pauses and a card in the middle of the screen tells what she saw and where to go now. Nothing moves until you close it (the button, Enter, or a tap); the clock and the police wait as well.

The yellow arrows. From then on yellow arrows lie on the road, one every 5 m in your lane, and show the way through the streets to the gate, round every corner. They are worked out again as you drive, so after a wrong turn they simply show a new way. A pulse runs along them towards the gate. The police keep chasing you while you wait for her.

The finale. Drive through the gate and the picture fades: you are on the 25 de Abril bridge with Marta beside you, driving south over the Tejo at about 86 km/h while the first light of the morning comes up in the east. There is nothing more to do: no police, no helicopter, heroic music (brass, strings and timpani, about 14 seconds, on the music volume). Then the result card. The drive over the bridge does not count towards your time. In the story, it ends act 1.

The highway and the countryside keep their bonus shards (+4 s each).

The city map: Lisbon's Baixa

The Baixa is the centre of Lisbon, rebuilt as a street grid after the 1755 earthquake. The map is a simplified version of it: the blocks are square, and the grid has 7 streets each way. The real Baixa has a few more, so some are left out. The real Baixa is also flat; the hills on this map are a game decision.

  • You start on Rua Augusta, facing south towards the Arco da Rua Augusta.
  • Squares: the Rossio and the Praça da Figueira in the north, the Praça do Comércio by the river in the south. The statue on the Praça do Comércio stands in the middle of the road, so traffic passes on both sides.
  • Landmarks you can see but not reach: the river Tejo, the 25 de Abril bridge (you only cross it in the finale), the castle hill, and across the river the town of Almada: a quay, an ochre cliff and on top of it about 1,250 houses in the pastel colours of Lisbon: small two-storey houses with shutters, town houses with shops and iron balconies, and a few apartment blocks, with hipped tile roofs and windows that are lit at night. The houses stand in rows along streets, with umbrella pines and cypresses between them and all over the cliff. Above it stands Cristo Rei, the floodlit statue of Christ with outstretched arms on its tall four-pillar pedestal, facing Lisbon. The river moves: long swells and fine ripples, it mirrors the sky towards the horizon, and the lights of the far bank tremble on it as long streaks.   • Parks: there are three, which the real Baixa does not have. The story needs them for the sealed letter.
  • Houses: closed blocks of Pombaline houses, wall to wall, as the Baixa was rebuilt after 1755: a ground floor plus 3 to 5 floors (OpenStreetMap gives most buildings 5 levels), arched stone ground floors with old shop fronts, the tallest windows and the balconies on the first floor, limewash colours, and tile roofs with dormers. Tiled fronts are the exception, as they are in the real Baixa.
  • Real buildings in their place: the National Theatre and Rossio station north of the Rossio, the yellow arcades and corner towers of the Praça do Comércio, and the Sé, seen at the end of Rua da Conceição. Their position is simplified, and they are modelled freely, not measured.
  • It is always about 02:30 at night. The clock on the arch shows it.

How the map was made

The city is a hand-built model of the real Baixa, not map data loaded by the game.

  1. Reference. We used OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL) as the reference. From it we took the names of the streets and squares, the order in which they follow each other from west to east and from north to south, where the squares and the river front lie, the route of the tram loop, and the usual height of the buildings (most have five levels).
  2. Simplified layout. The real Baixa is a rectangular grid, rebuilt after the 1755 earthquake, so it fits a game well. We drew it as a regular grid of 7 × 7 streets: square blocks of 48 m and streets 14 m wide. The real blocks are longer than they are wide and vary in size, and there are a few more streets, so the order is real and the measurements are not.
  3. Written into the code. The street names and the grid are fixed in room-2-chase/js/core.js (the BAIXA table, with streetAt naming the street under the car). The game ships no map file and calls no map service or API while it runs. It needs no key and keeps working if a map service is down.
  4. Landmarks modelled by hand. The Arco da Rua Augusta, the Praça do Comércio, the Rossio with the National Theatre and the station, the Santa Justa lift, the Sé, the castle hill, the 25 de Abril bridge, Cristo Rei and Almada were modelled from how they look and placed where they roughly stand, not from measured plans. Some are changed on purpose: the bridge has a main span of 300 m instead of 1,013 m so it fits the view, the hills and the three parks were added for the game, and the finish gate stands at the south-west corner, towards the bridge.

Hills. All three maps have hills, up to about 20 % steep. The Arco, the river front and the two ends of the highway are level. Uphill your top speed falls by up to 16 % and gravity pulls you back, half as strongly as it pushes you downhill. Downhill your top speed rises by up to 22 % and you coast on. The truck feels slopes most, the motorcycle least. Brakes and rolling friction hold a stopped vehicle in place.

Smooth picture. If the game runs below 48 frames per second for 2 seconds, it lowers the resolution one step (85, 70, then 60 %). If that does not help, the step is taken back. After 20 seconds at 57 or more it goes back up, at most twice. The choice of Ultra or Balanced stays yours.

Helicopter. After 60 seconds on the run (or sooner with the fifth star), a police helicopter joins the chase for the rest of the run and hunts you with its searchlight. Its searchlight follows you at 94 % of the police speed. While you are in the light (within 9 m of its centre), the police drive 6 % faster, and in the city they see you from 170 m instead of 110 m. It cannot bust you or damage you. An undamaged car or motorcycle at full speed on a clear, level road stays ahead of the light; the truck, and a badly damaged car, cannot.

Radio

Seven stations, or off: Reggae, Techno, Hardstyle, Schlager, Sing-Along Pop, Classical and Heavy Metal. Choose in the menu, or at any time with R or the radio button. The music plays while you drive, and is silent in the menu and in pause.

All of it is composed and synthesised by the game, with no music files. The tunes are generated by the game. The classical station plays Bach and Beethoven, whose works are in the public domain.

  • Drive time. In the city this is the time used. On the highway and in the countryside it is the time used plus the bonus seconds the shards gave you, so collecting shards never makes a run look slower.

  • These limits are for the car. For the other vehicles the drive time counts multiplied by their speed factor (motorcycle 1.12, truck 0.78), so each can earn three stars.
  • More than 50 % damage costs one star, but never the last one.
  • The best drive time per map is saved in the browser. Saved values under 10 s or over 15 minutes are ignored, because no run can take that long or that little.

Drivers and the leaderboard

  • Driver. In the main menu (section 03) you create a driver by typing a name, or pick a saved one. A name keeps letters, digits, spaces and . _ ' -, up to 16 characters; capital letters do not matter, so alex and Alex are the same driver.
  • Leaderboards. Every finished free ride (Lisbon, Highway, Countryside) goes on two boards of its map under the active driver: the all-time board and the board of the current calendar month. Each driver has one entry per board, their best time; a slower run never replaces it. The fastest time is first, and on a tie the time that was there first stays ahead. When a new month begins, the monthly board starts empty; the all-time board stays.
  • Leader of the month. The fastest driver of the current month on a map is named above the two boards.
  • Where they are shown. In the main menu (for the map that is chosen) and on the end screen after the finish. Each board shows the top five, and the driver's own place below them if they are further down. The end screen says whether the run is a new personal best and which place it has on both boards. It is set in large type: the drive time is the one big number, with the best time on this device under it, the stars beside the title, and time left (or shards), damage and wrecked police cars next to it. Without a driver, the end screen asks for a name first; the time is saved as soon as the name is entered.
  • Runs that end busted, wrecked or out of time, and the story mode, do not go on the board.
  • The drivers and the boards are kept in this browser only. There is no password and nothing is sent anywhere, so the board shows the people who play on the same device.
