The whole game in one paragraph

In April 2012 the Starfall necklace, lent by the Ashford family to Lisbon's naval museum for the centenary of the Titanic, vanished from its case. You were the detective on the case, until the trail ended at your own door. Now you are inmate 4471, convicted of stealing it. Room 1: you have ten minutes in a prison washroom to find the code for a hatch in the floor. If you get out (Ending A), a note shows that the guard wanted you out, and a car is waiting at the end of the tunnel. Room 2: the police chase begins. You drive a getaway vehicle through Lisbon's Baixa, collecting the evidence that proves you innocent while up to four police cars and a helicopter hunt you, and get out over the 25 de Abril bridge.

One night, one look

  • Time: both rooms are set on the same night: room 1 around 02:15, room 2 from about 02:30.
  • Design: both rooms share one design system: the same background, glass panels, fonts, buttons and cards. Each room keeps its own accent colour: orange in the prison, cyan on the run. Hale's radio messages in room 2 are orange because he comes from room 1.
  • A test (tests/project/page.test.js) checks that the shared design tokens and the story links between the rooms stay in place.

How the two rooms connect: the heist

  • The start page has one main button, Start the heist, which opens the heist page (heist/). Below it, each room can still be played on its own.
  • The driver name comes first, and it counts in both rooms. The heist page asks for a driver name before anything is played: type a name and press Start the heist, and you are on the online leaderboards of room 2 and of the heist, with no email and no password (the same account and the same rules for names as in room 2; a name someone else has is refused). If the online board can't be reached, the name becomes a driver of this device. A name from an earlier visit is offered again (Not you? asks for another). Play without a name starts at once; room 2 then asks for a name on its end screen.
  • Both rooms are on that one page. Room 1 is played in a frame, and while you play it, room 2 is loaded in a second frame under it, out of sight, down to the garage, and then stands still. When you leave room 1 through the tunnel door, room 2 is simply shown: no page is loaded at that moment, so there is nothing to wait for. A phone has too little memory for both 3D worlds at once: there the heist page only takes the name and then opens room 1 as its own page, and room 2 loads when room 1 is left, behind the light of the headlights. The same happens on a computer if room 2 should not be ready in time.
  • Ending A of room 1 hands over the escape time (the time used on the clock, wrong codes included) and leads into room 2 without a stop: when the door at the end of the tunnel swings open, the headlights behind it fill the screen and room 2 opens by itself in the same light. There is no card and nothing to click. Ending B stays in room 1 (try again).
  • Room 2 then skips its menu: the light clears and the old plank door of the tunnel swings open and you walk out into the garage, with the three vehicles across the hall. A message shows the escape time. From there it is a free ride through Lisbon.
  • When that drive is finished, heist time = escape time + drive time. The escape does not change the drive in any way.
  • The heist time goes on the heist leaderboard (each driver's best heist, fastest first), on this device and, when signed in, online. See room 2, The whole heist.
  • The two pages stay separate. The escape time travels in the browser's storage and is checked by the rules when it is read: a broken, impossible or old one (over six hours) is refused and room 2 opens its menu as usual.

Room 1 · Plot Twist: Cell Block D

This document describes the rules in room-1-escape/js/rules.js. Values marked (open) are not decided yet.

Goal

Open the hatch in the floor of the toilet stall and climb out before the clock reaches zero.

Story

The briefing is a short series of cards; Next and Back move between them.

  1. The front page. The Lisbon Times of Monday 16 April 2012: the Starfall necklace, lent by the Ashford family to the Museu de Marinha for an exhibition marking 100 years since the Titanic sank, vanished from its glass case on the night of the centenary. No alarm sounded, and a detective was put on the case.
  2. Who you are. Three weeks ago you were that detective. The trail ended at your own door: the necklace's velvet box was hidden in your car. Tonight you are inmate 4471, convicted of the theft: someone framed you. Your oldest friend on the force kept digging and found the proof that clears your name, but the people who framed you came after him. Before he disappeared from Lisbon, he hid the evidence across the city, in places only you would know to look.
  3. The door. Officer Hale walks you to washroom 2. In a short scene in the room you turn round to the open door, see him in the corridor light, and watch him swing the door shut and lock it. Through the door he says: "Your ten minutes have begun. Not a second more." He waits outside.
  4. The rumour. Inmates whisper about a hatch in the floor of this washroom, locked with a four-digit code. Three spots in the room hide a car key, the first two digits and the last two digits of the code, each behind a short Titanic mini game.
  5. How to play: the controls.

The clock only starts when the player presses Start the clock after the briefing.

You walk freely through the washroom in the first person. Where you stand decides your scene: the stall (inside it or right in front of its doorway), the sinks (the west end of the room) or the door (everywhere else). You have to be well inside a new part of the room before it counts, so walking along a border never switches back and forth. The description of a scene is shown the first time you walk in. The Hatch is the only scene you do not walk into: in the stall you click the hatch (or its marker) to kneel down at the lock, and any step stands you up again. Walls, the sink counter, the bin, the bucket, the stall partitions, its open door and the toilet block your way.

You see your own hands at the bottom of the picture: real hand models with a joint in every finger, in the black and sand stripes of the prison uniform. At rest they hang relaxed and never quite still: they breathe, drift a little, and now and then make a small gesture of their own (a loose fist, a tap of the index finger, a stretch of the fingers, the thumb rubbing the fingertips). As you walk, the arms swing like in a real walk, opposite to each other, every swing a little different; the wrists trail behind the arms and the fingers close a little on the back swing and open on the forward swing. Your head dips at every footfall, a little more or less with each step, and sways gently from one foot to the other. You hear every footfall: real recordings of hard shoes on the concrete floor (the heel, then the sole), a splash when you step into a puddle and boots on the wooden rungs of the ladder, each step a little different, with the echo of the bare concrete room. The arms trail behind when you turn your head, and the right hand opens and reaches out whenever you use something. If the hand models cannot load (for example when the page is opened straight from the folder), simpler drawn hands stand in. They never disappear into a wall, and they are hidden during the endings, except while you walk through the tunnel after Ending A. They change nothing in the rules.

Look down and you see yourself, the way you would in real life: the front of the striped shirt with its buttons, your belt line, your legs in the striped trousers and your leather work boots on the floor, and your hands hanging at your sides. Your head bends forward on your neck as you look down, so your eyes come forward over your chest, and you breathe. The feet stay planted where they stand: as you walk, one foot swings past the other at every footfall you hear, rolling off the ball of the foot and landing on the heel, the knees bend and the arms swing opposite to the legs; a step back or to the side steps that way, and turning on the spot makes you shuffle your feet round. Kneeling at the hatch you sit back on your heels with your knees on the floor and your hands on your thighs, and the right hand still reaches out to the lock. Your body throws a soft shadow on the floor away from the tube light (and from the stall lamp inside the stall), sharp at the boots and blurred higher up. The steel mirror only shows your hands, as before. All of this changes nothing in the rules.

On a laptop or desktop you only walk with the arrow keys; there are no buttons for it. A touch screen has no arrow keys, so there (and only there) "Walk to" buttons glide you to a fixed spot in another scene. Walking anywhere else is refused: "You cannot get there from here."

Clues and the code

There are exactly three clues, one behind each of three spots. Clicking a spot opens a mini game of at most 30 seconds. Winning it gives the clue; losing it (or running out of the 30 seconds) gives nothing, and the same game can be tried again at once. Giving up (Esc or Give up), pausing or an ending stops the game without a result.

The first time a mini game comes up, a card explains it before it starts: the goal, how it works step by step, the controls (mouse and keyboard, or touch on a touch screen), the 30 seconds and the prize. The game and its 30 seconds start only with Start the game (or Enter); Back to the room (or Esc) closes the card, and it comes up again next time. The game clock keeps running while you read. After you have started a game once, it opens at once: when you try again after a loss or come back after giving up. A new game shows every card again.

  • A won clue is kept in your clue notes and can be read again at its spot; its mini game cannot be played again.
  • The graffiti on the stall wall ("She was unsinkable. She sailed only once. The year opens the floor.") is a hint, not one of the three clues.

The nine mini games

At the start of every game, three different games out of nine are dealt at random to the three spots (tile, mirror, scratches), so every round plays differently. The rules check that the three are different; the room deals them (MiniGames.pickGames in room-1-escape/js/minigames.js).

GameHow to win in 30 seconds
BattleshipTwo 6 × 6 grids, three ships each (3, 2 and 2 cells). As in the real game, ships never touch, not even at a corner; once a ship is sunk, the water around it is marked and cannot be fired at. Fire at the enemy waters; a hit lets you fire again. Sink all enemy ships before the bot sinks yours. The bot fires at random or, half the time, next to one of its hits.
Spot the differenceTwo pictures of the Titanic at night; the right one has 5 changes, chosen from 9. Click all five, in either picture. A wrong click costs 2 seconds.
Tic-tac-toeYour anchors against the bot's icebergs, you start. Three in a row wins, a draw starts a new round. The bot always takes a winning cell, blocks three times out of four, likes the centre, otherwise plays at random.
Deck runAs a sailor of the Titanic, run to the bow on the icy night of 14 April 1912: first along the side of the ship past its round portholes, then out on the open deck past the railing with its life rings, the lifeboats, a funnel and the foremast. Jump (Space, ↑ or a click) over crates, steamer trunks, barrels and blocks of ice. Each obstacle knocks you down exactly where it is drawn. Three knocks and you are out.
Deck climbAs the same sailor, bounce up through the Titanic: the engine room (boilers, furnaces, pipes), the crew quarters (bunks and lockers), second class (oak-panelled corridors and the library) and first class, the longest level, twice as tall as the others (the Grand Staircase with its glass dome, chandeliers, the clock "Honour and Glory" and the bronze cherub). The game is won the moment you land on the boat deck with its funnels and lifeboats above first class; there is nothing to climb above it. Steer with ← → or the mouse. Lifebuoys bounce you higher. Falling back down loses.
Lifeboat jumpThe Titanic goes down by the bow and her stern rises out of the sea. As the same sailor as in the deck run and the deck climb, jump from the stern three times, onto a lifeboat, a collapsible boat and a cork raft (150, 120 and 96 pixels wide), each smaller than the last, while the stern rises higher with every jump. A power meter swings from short to far; Space, ↑ or a click (a tap) stops it and you jump. In the air, steer a little with ← → (A D) or by pressing the left or right half of the picture. The boats drift slowly; a lantern on a pole marks the middle of each. A landing in the middle scores 100 points, on the edge 40, in the sea 0. 150 points win; the game ends as soon as 150 can no longer be reached. A fair player is done in about 12 seconds.
PinballA vintage White Star Line table in walnut, cream, brass and burgundy. Score 1,500 points with three balls. Flippers: ← and → (A and D), Space for both, or press the left or right half of the table; the ball shoots by itself (or with Space). Points: pop bumper 150, lifebuoy target 250, slingshot 10, each R-M-S lane 100 and all three lanes 1,000 more, and 250 for every flipper shot that reaches the crow's nest bell at the top. A fair player gets there in about 15 seconds; losing all three balls loses.
HackingBreak into an old control machine in the Titanic's engine room, a riveted steel panel with brass plates, lamps and leaking steam: two parts, each played once, before the firewall burning in its furnace locks you out. Part 1: on the indicator card (cream paper on a brass drum) change the frequency (← →, A D) and the amplitude (↑ ↓, W S) of your red ink line until it lies on the dashed blue master line (a sync reading shows how close you are), then lock it with Space; on a touch screen tap the brass buttons under the card. Part 2: four key plates with brass pegs (6 × 6), only one matches the master key plate peg for peg, each decoy has two pegs changed; choose with ← → and Space or click it. The firewall burns up by itself in 16 seconds and every wrong lock or wrong plate adds 15 %; full means locked out. Winning shows FULL AHEAD. A fair player gets in in about 8 seconds.
ConductingThe night the Titanic sank, her band played on to the end. You conduct it in the first-class dining saloon (white Jacobean panelling, leaded windows glowing amber, tables laid with white cloths and rose-shaded lamps), which tilts more and more as the ship goes down while its chandeliers still hang plumb. The five musicians are sailors in the style of the deck run and the deck climb (navy jumpers with the square striped collar, round caps with ribbons): two violins and a viola on the left, a cello and a double bass on the right, you in tails in front, seen from behind with the baton. The band plays the opening of Bach's Brandenburg Concerto No. 3 (a CC0 recording, room-1-escape/sounds/orchestra.mp3, a steady 117 beats a minute). The first four beats are counted in (1, 2, 3, 4, strokes there are free); then bring the baton down on every beat with Space, ↑ (W), a click or a tap, just as the golden ring closes on the baton's tip. A stroke within 0.18 s of the beat counts (within 0.08 s it is PERFECT); a beat with no stroke, or a stroke off the music (too early, or a second one), puts the band out of time. 10 beats in time win (about 7 seconds without a miss); 12 out of time lose. If the recording has not loaded, a plain plucked beat at the same tempo plays instead; the volume and mute of the room apply.

The lock

  • Four wheels, each showing 0–9. Up after 9 is 0, down after 0 is 9.
  • You can only turn the wheels and try the code while kneeling at the hatch.
  • Try the code:

- Right code: the hatch opens, Ending A. - Wrong code: "Clunk. The lock holds." Hale hears the noise, and you lose 30 seconds. The number of wrong codes is counted.

The clock has stopped: the walk costs no time. The door only opens from right in front of it; clicked from further away it is refused: "The door is at the far end of the tunnel. Walk up to it first." The earlier decision: jamming the door early gives one more minute at the very end. That can turn Ending B into Ending A.

After Ending B, every action is refused except Try again, which starts a new game from the briefing. After Ending A the game goes on in room 2; opening room 1 again starts a new game.

The escape time (Ending A only). It is the time used on the clock: ten minutes less what is left; if you got out during the extra minute behind the jammed door, the ten minutes plus what was used of that minute. A wrong code costs 30 s on the clock, so it counts. The walk through the tunnel costs nothing, as the clock has stopped. The time is kept in the browser's storage for room 2, where the drive is added to it. Ending B and an unfinished game hand nothing over.

Saving

  • The game is saved in the browser after every accepted action and every few seconds while the clock runs.
  • After a reload, the player can Continue (with the time that was left) or start a New game, which asks for confirmation first.
  • A saved game is checked field by field when loaded. Anything that is not a real save is ignored, and a new game starts.

Room 2 · The Chase

This document describes the rules in room-2-chase/js/core.js, and the pickups and police in room-2-chase/js/game.js.

Where room 2 starts

Room 2 begins where room 1 ends. You (inmate 4471, the framed detective) have climbed out of the tunnel under Cell Block D. It is about 02:30 at night. In a lock-up garage, Officer Hale's getaway vehicle is waiting. Every police car in town is now hunting the escaped inmate.

Officer Hale is a secret ally. He believes you were framed, but he cannot prove it. In the story mode he helps over the radio; his messages are shown in orange, the colour of room 1. Police dispatch messages are red.

The garage (Lisbon)

A run in Lisbon begins in that garage, 30 m under the Baixa, and on foot. You are the same person as in room 1: you walk with the arrow keys or W A S D, briskly (3.4 m/s), and run while you hold Shift (5.6 m/s), turn your head by dragging, and see your own hands in the striped sleeves; the walk, the head movement, the hands and the echo of your steps are room 1's. It begins where room 1 ended: in the tunnel from Cell Block D, behind its old plank door. The tunnel is built as in room 1 and from the same scanned surfaces (stone walls, a brick vault, old pavers, the wooden ladder at its far end, two old bulbs). The door swings open and you walk out through it into the garage by yourself, in under three seconds; then you have the controls (with reduced motion you stand in the garage at once, the door open behind you). The doorway is in the wall beside the ramp, and the three getaway vehicles stand across the hall in their bays, numbered 01 to 03 on the far wall, under rows of strip lights: the moonlight of the city does not reach down here.

  • Choose: walk up to a vehicle, or tap it on the card (you then walk to its driver's door). The one you stand at is the chosen one. This is the same choice as in the menu, so the menu's vehicle is the one marked when you arrive.
  • Get in: you run to the driver's door. Your right hand hooks its fingers under the handle and pulls it out of its pocket, the latch clicks, the door comes open with the hand, and you step back as it swings wide; you get in through it, head down under the roof, onto the seat, take the wheel with both hands, and your left hand pulls the door shut. Only now is an engine heard: on foot the garage is quiet apart from your steps. For a moment you sit in the cabin: in the coupe a dashboard with two dials under a hood, the screen of the navigation with the route through the old town, the centre stack with vents, the climate panel and the start button, the gear lever, leather bucket seats and a small bench behind them; in the truck a high dashboard, the hump of the engine between two seats and the bunk behind. The engine starts, the lights and the dials come on, and the needles sweep once to the end of their dials and back. The vehicle rolls out of its bay, still seen from the seat, and then the view draws back out of the cabin to its place behind the vehicle. The motorcycle has no door: you swing onto the seat and take the bars. Esc at the wheel puts you back on your feet beside the door.
  • Drive out: drive up the ramp (at most 32 km/h down here). Walls, pillars and the two parked vehicles stop you, but a bump costs only speed, never damage. Over the top of the ramp the vehicle drives itself: a roller door rolls up, and you drive straight out onto the street, with no cut and no fade, across the pavement and round to the left into the lane. The door is in the house north-east of the usual start, on the cross street, and every run in Lisbon can see it there. As soon as you are on the street the map of the evidence opens.
  • Nothing counts in the garage: the clock does not run, no police come, and the HUD is hidden. The run starts with Start driving on the map, as before.
  • Skip the garage goes straight to the street, to the usual start. Try again and Restart skip it too. The story begins in the garage as well.

Room 2 plays no sound files, so the steps here are synthesised, where room 1 plays recordings.

  • There is one map, Lisbon. (The highway and the countryside of earlier versions, and the story acts played on them, were removed.)
  • A piece of evidence is picked up within 3.8 m; a repair kit within 3.6 m, and only when you have damage.
  • Lisbon has traffic lights, 26 other cars, two white city buses, four motorcycles and three yellow trams. The trams only run on their tracks, a stone track bed with two rails set into the road: a clockwise loop through Rua da Conceição, Rua da Prata, the north side of the Praça do Comércio and Rua Áurea. As on real Lisbon streets, cars and the player can drive over the tracks. Each wheel that rolls over a rail gives a jolt: the picture and the vehicle shake, the rail clacks, and phones that can vibrate give a short buzz. The faster you go, the harder the jolt; the front wheels hit first and the rear ones a moment later. Driving along the stone track bed rumbles lightly. The trams themselves run on steel railway wheels with a flange on the inside, on a two-axle truck with springs and brake blocks. The buses are 12 m long, so at a crossing they go straight on or turn left in the wide arc, never right into the tight corner (unless that is the only way left at the edge of the map). Everything stops at red lights and keeps its distance to the back of the vehicle ahead, so nobody runs into the long tail of a bus or a tram.
  • Other vehicles at night: every vehicle throws its headlights on the road ahead, so a car waiting at a dark crossing can be seen from far away. Its bright brake lights are on while it slows down or stands. You can crash into buses and motorcycles like into any other vehicle, and a hit pushes the other vehicle away by weight: a motorcycle (weight 0.25) flies off, two cars (1 each) both give way, a bus or coach (4) or a lorry (3.5) hardly moves and you bounce off it; a tram on its rails does not move at all. Your motorcycle weighs 0.3, your truck 3.5. A hit off the middle turns the other vehicle; it slides at most 14 m, stops at walls, lamp posts and parked cars, and drives back into its lane once its driver has recovered. You keep more of your speed the lighter the vehicle you hit, and each hit sounds like what you hit: a motorcycle clatters, a bus booms.
  • Parked cars: the cars parked along the kerbs in Lisbon can be pushed away too. Their handbrake is on, so they count 1.6 times their weight (a car 1.6, an SUV about 2.1): a hit at city speed shoves one a few metres, and it turns when you hit it off its middle. It stops at houses, lamp posts and other parked cars (and pushes those on), never more than 7 m from its place, and stays where it comes to rest until the next run. You keep more speed than against a wall, but the hit still counts as a crash. Traffic stops behind a parked car that has been pushed into its lane.
  • Each map has an edge you cannot cross. When you near it the screen turns red and a "turn back" arrow appears. In Lisbon the outer streets are part of the map, so there this only happens when you head for the edge.

In Lisbon you do not collect star shards. Your oldest friend on the force found out who really broke into the naval museum and hid the proof across the Baixa (room 1 tells this in its briefing). There are six pieces, one of each, in random places every run. Each floats above the street with a glow, and the panel at the top left shows one icon per piece that lights up when it is in the car.

While you drive an arrow on the screen (at the bottom in the middle; on an upright phone under the pursuit panel) points to the nearest piece still out there, with its name and distance; once all six are found it turns gold and points to the witness, and once she is in the car, to the gate. (The story's sealed letter is on neither.)

The witness. With all six pieces in the car, one thing is still missing: a person. Marta Silva, a night-shift nurse, saw the real thief climb out of the museum window, and she will say so in court. She waits on a pavement somewhere in the Baixa, 70 to 200 m from where you are, with a golden light over her head. She turns to face your car and waves: now and then while you are far away, and without stopping once you are closer than 45 m. The radar shows her in gold (an arrow at the rim while she is out of range), the signal bars point to her, and a seventh icon appears in the panel. Stop next to her, closer than 7 m and slower than about 11 km/h, and she runs to the passenger door on the right. Once the vehicle stands still, she opens the door, steps in (up the steps into the truck's cab), sits down and pulls the door shut; on the motorcycle she climbs onto the pillion seat. Getting in takes about one second, and the vehicle waits for her. From then on she rides along beside you, visible through the window, over the bridge as well. Drive off before she reaches the vehicle (more than 12 m away) and she goes back to wait. With her on board, the golden gate opens at the south-west corner of the Baixa, where Rua do Crucifixo meets Avenida Infante Dom Henrique by the river. It faces the 25 de Abril bridge, and driving through it takes you onto the bridge.

The story card. As soon as Marta has shut the door, the run pauses and a card in the middle of the screen tells what she saw and where to go now. Nothing moves until you close it (the button, Enter, or a tap); the clock and the police wait as well.

The yellow arrows. From then on yellow arrows lie on the road, one every 5 m in your lane, and show the way through the streets to the gate, round every corner. They are worked out again as you drive, so after a wrong turn they simply show a new way. A pulse runs along them towards the gate. The police keep chasing you while you wait for her.

The finale. Behind the gate starts the approach road to the bridge, built after the real one: a concrete viaduct on tall twin piers that runs west along the river, passes under the side span and climbs round in a banked loop to the deck, 42 m up. Drive through the gate and the game takes the wheel for good: the clock stops, the police give up, and the vehicle drives itself up the approach road to the bridge, on the side of the road it came through the gate on. The keys, the touch pads and the tilt do nothing from here on. If it comes through the gate at an angle or very fast it may still touch a concrete parapet (scraping along one throws sparks, a hard knock slows it down); from 90 m on the barrier in the middle keeps it on its side. The long straight along the river is not shown in full: 2.5 seconds behind the gate the picture cuts, as in a film, to a helicopter shot high outside the loop, where your vehicle is now 25 m before the curve, in its lane and at its speed (at least 65 km/h). The camera follows it into the loop for 4 seconds and then cuts back behind it. A vehicle that is already close to the loop is not moved. On the deck the drive goes on without a cut: you are on the 25 de Abril bridge with Marta beside you, driving south over the Tejo, sliding over into the right-hand lane, while the first light of the morning comes up in the east. The heroic music (brass, strings and timpani, about 14 seconds, on the music volume) starts 80 m before the deck, while you are still coming up the loop. From then on the engine fades out bit by bit under it, over 6 seconds, until only the music is left (the vehicle still cruises at about 86 km/h, changes down and accelerates to about 125 km/h). There is nothing more to do: no police, no helicopter. Then the result card. The drive over the bridge does not count towards your time. In the story, it ends act 1.

The city map: Lisbon's Baixa

The Baixa is the centre of Lisbon, rebuilt as a street grid after the 1755 earthquake. The map is a simplified version of it: the blocks are square, and the grid has 7 streets each way. The real Baixa has a few more, so some are left out. The real Baixa is also flat; the hills on this map are a game decision.

How the map was made

The city is a hand-built model of the real Baixa, not map data loaded by the game.

  1. Reference. We used OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL) as the reference. From it we took the names of the streets and squares, the order in which they follow each other from west to east and from north to south, where the squares and the river front lie, the route of the tram loop, and the usual height of the buildings (most have five levels).
  2. Simplified layout. The real Baixa is a rectangular grid, rebuilt after the 1755 earthquake, so it fits a game well. We drew it as a regular grid of 7 × 7 streets: square blocks of 48 m and streets 14 m wide. The real blocks are longer than they are wide and vary in size, and there are a few more streets, so the order is real and the measurements are not.
  3. Written into the code. The street names and the grid are fixed in room-2-chase/js/core.js (the BAIXA table, with streetAt naming the street under the car). The game ships no map file and calls no map service or API while it runs. It needs no key and keeps working if a map service is down.
  4. Landmarks modelled by hand. The Arco da Rua Augusta, the Praça do Comércio, the Rossio with the National Theatre and the station, the Santa Justa lift, the Sé, the castle hill, the 25 de Abril bridge, Cristo Rei and Almada were modelled from how they look and placed where they roughly stand, not from measured plans. Some are changed on purpose: the bridge has a main span of 300 m instead of 1,013 m so it fits the view, the hills and the three parks were added for the game, and the finish gate stands at the south-west corner, towards the bridge.

  • Leaderboards. Every finished free ride goes on two boards under the active driver: the all-time board and the board of the current calendar month.   • Leader of the month. The fastest driver of the current month on a map is named above the two boards.
  • Where they are shown. In the main menu (for the map that is chosen) and on the end screen after the finish. The whole heist (room 1 and room 2 as one time)

  • How it starts. Only from room 1: after Ending A it opens room 2 by itself and brings the escape time. Room 2 opens in the light of the headlights room 1 ended in; the light clears and you stand in the garage, with no menu and no card in between. A message shows the escape time. Main menu on the garage card gives the heist up. As the page was not opened by a click, the sound (and on a phone the motion sensor) starts with your first key or tap.
  • What counts. The heist is the escape followed by a finished free ride through Lisbon, with any vehicle. Heist time = escape time + drive time. The escape time changes nothing in the drive: the same clock, police and evidence as in every Lisbon run.
  • Trying again. If the drive fails (busted, wrecked, out of time), Drive again keeps the heist open, and only the finished drive counts. Going back to the menu ends the heist. Once a heist is finished, the escape time is used up; a new heist starts in room 1.
  • The heist leaderboard. Shown on the end screen above the board of the map: escape + drive = heist time, under it the best heist on this device (or that this one is the new best) and, signed in, the online record with its driver, then your place, and the top five heists (your own row below them if you are further down), each with its two parts and the vehicle. Each driver keeps one entry, their best heist; a slower one never replaces it, and on a tie the heist that was there first stays ahead. Signed in, it is everybody's board online; otherwise it is the board of this device. The drive also goes on the Lisbon leaderboard as usual.
  • Refused. An escape time that is broken, under 1 s, over 11 minutes (room 1 cannot last longer), from the future or older than six hours is ignored and the menu opens. Opening room 2 without coming from room 1 never starts a heist. Online, the database accepts only the two parts (escape 1 to 660 s, drive 10 to 900 s), adds them up itself, and takes at most one heist every 10 seconds from an account.
  • What cannot be checked. The escape time comes from the player's own browser. The rules above keep it possible, but a player who changes their browser's storage can claim a faster escape than they played. The same is true of every time on the boards.
  • Online. The boards everybody sees are kept in the game's online database. Every run is also kept on this device; when the online board can't be reached, the game says so and shows the boards of this device instead.
